# Tenders: inviting bids

*Who can use this: Content manager and Administrator.*

**Tenders** is where you post the notices you invite bids on. Each one gets
its own page on your website with the tender number, what you are buying, the
closing date and the documents to download.

Bidders submit the way the notice tells them — by email, by post, or on a
portal you name. **Nothing is submitted through your website**, so there is no
queue of bids here to work through.

A tender that has closed stays on your site as a record of what you have
tendered for. It is marked **Closed**, and the Tenders page keeps it under
the **Closed** filter.

## Posting a tender

Press **New tender**. The form has five tabs.

1. **Content** — the tender number, the title, a one-sentence summary and the
   scope of work. The status, the category and whether it is featured are in
   the column beside them.
2. **Dates and fees** — when it opens, the last date for submission, and the
   optional pre-bid meeting, bid opening date, document fee and EMD.
3. **Documents** — the tender pack.
4. **How to bid** — where a bid is sent, and who to ask about the tender.
5. **SEO** — the page's title and description for search engines, as on every
   other page.

Nothing is on your website until you set the status to **Published**.

### The tender number

Type it exactly as you issue it — `TW/2026/014`. It is kept as typed, because
it is what a bidder quotes back to you.

**No two tenders may have the same number.** If you try, the form says so.

Bidders can search for a tender by its number and the punctuation does not
matter: `TW/2026/014`, `TW 2026 014` and `tw2026014` all find it.

### The dates

Every time you enter is in your own timezone, and every bidder sees it in
your timezone whatever their computer is set to — so a deadline of six in the
evening is six in the evening for everybody.

- **Opens for bids** and **Last date for submission** are both needed.
- **Pre-bid meeting** is optional. If you give a time, give a place with it.
- **Bids opened** is optional, and must be on or after the closing date.

The page shows **Open**, **Closing soon**, **Closed** or **Opens soon** by
itself, from these dates and the clock. There is nothing to switch over when a
tender closes: it closes on the minute you said, on its own, and the page says
so even to somebody who had it open beforehand.

A tender is marked **Closing soon** inside three days of its closing date.

### The fees

Both are in **rupees**, and both are optional.

- **Leave it blank** if your notice does not state one. Nothing is shown.
- **Enter 0** for a tender document that is free. The page reads *Free*.

## The documents

On the **Documents** tab, press **Add a document** for each file in the pack:
the notice itself, the bill of quantities, a corrigendum. Up to ten.

Each one is a file from your media library — browse for it, or upload it in
the dialog. Give it a name a bidder will understand ("Tender document", "Bill
of quantities") and, if it helps, a one-line note ("Read this first").

Use the arrows to put them in the order a bidder should read them.

**These files are public**, like the notice itself — anybody may bid, so
anybody may download the pack. Do not put anything confidential here.

If a file is ever deleted from the media library, the document disappears
from the website rather than leaving a broken button, and this screen marks
the row so you can fix it.

**Deleting a tender does not delete its documents.** They stay in your media
library, because the same pack is often attached to two tenders.

## How a bid is submitted

Fill in at least one of the three:

- **Email a bid to** — an address.
- **Bidding portal** — a link, if bids go through one.
- **Post or deliver to** — the address a sealed bid is sent to. Line breaks
  are kept as you type them.

**You cannot publish a tender with none of the three.** A notice nobody can
answer is worse than no notice, so the form refuses it and says so.

Underneath, name **who to ask about this tender** — a person, a telephone
number and an email address. All optional; the page leaves out what you have
not filled in.

## Categories

**Tenders → Categories** is a short list: Networking, Annual maintenance,
whatever suits your work. It is what the filter on your Tenders page offers.

A tender sits in one category, or none.

- **Switch a category off** and it disappears from the website, along with
  every tender in it. Nothing is deleted.
- **Delete a category** and its tenders stay, filed under none. Find them
  again with the **Filed nowhere** filter on the Tenders list.

A category with nothing published in it is not offered as a filter, so a
visitor never presses one and gets an empty page.

## Next year's tender

Press **Duplicate** on a tender. You get a draft copy with the same scope,
fees and documents.

Two things to change on the copy: the **tender number**, which is given a
`-copy` suffix so you notice it, and the **dates**.

## Putting tenders on another page

A tender's notice always has its own page. You can also show the open ones
somewhere else:

- In the **page builder**, add a **Cards** section and choose **Open tenders**
  as its source. Only open tenders are shown, closing soonest first — a row of
  closed tenders would invite a bid nobody can make.
- In a **menu**, add either the **Tenders** site section (the whole list) or a
  single tender. A menu entry disappears on its own if you unpublish the
  tender; a closed one stays, because its page still works.

## Sharing a draft before you publish

Press **Share a draft** on the tender. You get a private link anybody can open
without signing in — for a colleague or a legal review — that expires when you
say. The page looks exactly as it will when published.

## Page history

Every save is kept. Press **History** on a tender to see what changed and when,
to look at an older version, or to load one back into the form. Loading one
back does not publish it: you still press Save.
