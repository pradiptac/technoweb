# Careers: vacancies and applications

*Who can use this: Vacancies — Content manager and Administrator. Applications — Support engineer and Administrator.*

The **Careers** section of the sidebar has two halves, deliberately held by
different roles: **Vacancies** (the job adverts, which are public content) and
**Applications** (the CVs people send, which are private personal data). A
person who edits the website's content does not automatically see candidates'
CVs.

## Vacancies

**Careers → Vacancies** holds your job adverts. They appear on your website's
Careers page, each with its own page and an application form, and each page
carries the job-posting information Google's job search reads.

To post a vacancy:

1. Press **New vacancy**.
2. On the **Content** tab, enter the **Job title**, a **Summary** and **About
   the role**.
3. On **The role** tab, enter the **Team or department**, **Location**,
   **Employment type** (Full time, Part time, Contract, Internship,
   Temporary), the number of **Openings** and the **Experience level**; write
   **What they will do** and **What we are looking for** (one per line); and
   tick the **Accepted qualifications**.
4. Optionally enter a salary range on the same tab (**Salary from**,
   **Salary to**, **Currency**, and **Per** year or month). Leave it blank if
   you prefer not to publish one.
5. Back on **Content**, set **Applications close** if the role has a closing
   date.
6. Set **Status** to *Published* and press **Create vacancy** (**Save
   vacancy** when editing). The **SEO** tab works as on any
   page (chapter 13).

- **A blank location means the job is remote.** Search engines need one or the
  other, so the site treats "no location" as remote. Always fill it in for an
  office-based role.
- **The closing date closes the vacancy by itself**: after it, the vacancy
  disappears from the list, its page stops answering, and the form refuses new
  applications — even from somebody who opened the page earlier.
- The page shows "What happens next" and your careers email address from
  **System → Settings → Contact** (*Careers email*), or the support address if
  that is blank.

### Qualifications and experience levels

The lists of **qualifications** and **experience levels** offered on the
vacancy form are yours to edit: press **Qualifications** on the Vacancies
screen — add "B.E. Computer Science" or "5–8 years" as you need. One that is
used by a vacancy cannot be deleted.

## Applications

**Careers → Applications** lists what candidates sent: name, email, phone,
current employer, years of experience, a cover letter, a portfolio link and a
**CV**. A button such as **4 unread** at the top opens the new ones.

- Filter by status; search by name, email, employer or role. The application
  count beside a vacancy on the Vacancies screen opens just that vacancy's
  applications.
- Open an application to read it and press **Download CV**. CVs are stored
  privately and can only be opened from the console — there is no public
  address for them.
- Move an application along: **New**, **Shortlisted**, **Interviewing**,
  **Offered**, **Hired** or **Not proceeding**, with a **Note** for colleagues
  (under **Move this along**).
  The candidate never sees the note.
- **Changing the status does not email the candidate.** Contact them yourself.

Candidates can upload a CV as PDF, Word (DOC, DOCX), RTF or OpenDocument, up
to 2 MB. Archives and other file types are refused.

Each new application is emailed to the careers address in **System → Settings
→ Contact** (*Careers email*; the support address if that is blank), and the
candidate gets an automatic acknowledgement.

Applications are kept even if the vacancy is later deleted.

## How long CVs are kept

Applications — and their CVs — are deleted automatically after **180 days** by
default. An administrator can change this under **System → Settings → Data
retention** (*Keep job applications for*), with a minimum of 30 days.
Deleting an application by hand deletes its CV at the same time.

Keep this period no longer than you need: a CV is personal data, and holding
strangers' CVs indefinitely is a liability, not an asset.

## Things to know

- Content managers post vacancies; support engineers (and administrators) read
  applications.
- A blank location is published as a remote role.
- The closing date switches the vacancy off by itself.
- Status changes are not emailed to candidates.
- CVs are deleted after the retention period — download any you need to keep
  under your own records policy.
