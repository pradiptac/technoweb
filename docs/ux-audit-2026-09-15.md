# UI/UX audit — 2026-09-15

A walk through the whole product on the night of 14–15 September: 34 routes
across the public site, the customer portal and the console, each looked at
in a browser at 1440px and 390px (136 frames), with the components behind
every interaction read alongside. Findings are ranked by how much they change
for the person using the screen, and each says what it would take to build.

Two things frame the list. First, the fundamentals are already unusually
strong for a site at this stage — the automated gates (`npm run audit`,
`audit:mobile`, `npm run themes`) hold every route to WCAG AA contrast, zero
overflow, one `h1`, 24px targets, reduced motion, and every form keeps what
was typed through a refusal. None of the findings below is a defect of that
kind; they are about what the product *does* when somebody acts, which no
gate measures. Second, the product is three tools with three tempos — a
site that is read, a portal that is checked, a console that is worked at for
hours — and the recommendations are split that way because the same idea is
worth different amounts in each.

Effort: **S** under half a day, **M** one to two days, **L** three days or
more. Each item names the backend work it needs, if any; most need none.

---

## What is already right, and should not be "improved"

Worth writing down so nobody spends the next pass on it.

- Every form is `<Form>` and survives a 422 with its values intact; errors
  land on the field and on the tab that owns it. The only gap is *timing*
  (see P8) — the errors are correct once they arrive.
- Modals are real `<dialog>`s; the drawer, the mega menu and the top-bar
  panel share one open/close contract; every carousel has a Pause; the
  reveal system is scoped under a JS-set attribute so no-JS and
  reduced-motion users get static content.
- Light, dark and system schemes, painted before first paint, with the
  console and the site remembering separately.
- The console's list screens become labelled cards on a phone; every filter
  bar is one component; every save has a sticky action bar; every
  destructive action is a red button with a confirmation.
- Toasts and alerts are two different things and both are used for the
  right job; two live regions, one per politeness.
- Search on the shop is a proper listbox with keyboard navigation; the
  checkout fills the address from a PIN code.

## Public site

**P1 — Filters wait for "Apply". Effort M. Backend: none.**
The catalogue (`/products/[category]`), the shop's filter bar and the search
page all collect a query, a brand and a sort into a form and submit on
Apply. Changing the brand dropdown does nothing until the button is pressed,
and the button is the fourth control in the row. Apply the change on
`change` for the selects (with `useTransition` so the results dim while they
load) and on a 300ms debounce for the text box, keeping the form's own
submit for no-JS and Enter. The URL stays the state, so nothing about
sharing or the back button changes. This is the single most-felt change on
the list: it is every product listing, every day.

**P2 — The header search has no suggestions. Effort M. Backend: none.**
`/search` already answers grouped results (products, guides, solutions) in
one call, ranked with part numbers first. The header box submits and waits.
A typeahead popover under the box — five rows per group, arrow keys, Enter
follows, Escape closes — turns the search from a page into a control. The
shop's `store-search.tsx` listbox is the pattern to reuse, not a second one.

**P3 — Category tiles that lead nowhere.** Effort S. Backend: none.
`/products` shows "Surveillance (0)" and "Accessories (0)": a tile whose
count says the listing is empty and still links to it. Either hide a
category with nothing published (the shop already does — "only categories
with something in them") or keep the tile and change its action: "Nothing
listed yet — ask us" opening the enquiry form with the category filled in.
The second is better for a catalogue that exists to start conversations.

**P4 — "Was this helpful?" on knowledge-base articles. Effort S+S. Backend:
one endpoint.** `knowledge_articles.helpful_count` exists, the index sorts by
it, and **nothing increments it** — there is no endpoint and no control. A
thumbs pair at the foot of an article, one vote per browser (localStorage
key by slug), `POST /knowledge-base/{slug}/helpful` throttled 10/min and
answering 204 always. It is the one signal that tells the support desk which
articles actually deflect tickets, which is what the knowledge base is for.

**P5 — Compare hardware side by side. Effort L. Backend: none.**
A hardware catalogue is browsed by comparing, and every product already
carries an ordered spec sheet (`SpecSheet`, order-preserving by design).
A "Compare" tick on catalogue cards, a floating tray holding up to three,
and a `/products/compare?ids=` page rendering the sheets as one table with
the rows aligned by key. Client-side selection in `sessionStorage`; the page
fetches the products it is given. This is the feature that makes the
catalogue better than a distributor's PDF.

**P6 — Long articles need a map. Effort M. Backend: none.**
Blog posts and knowledge articles are `Prose` from top to bottom with no
"On this page". Generate a sticky contents list from the `h2`s at render
(the HTML is already sanitised, so a cheap parse is safe), with the current
section highlighted by an `IntersectionObserver` — the reveal observer's
sibling. Pair it with a thin reading-progress bar in the article's own
colour; the route loader is already `scaleX`, so the primitive exists.

**P7 — Recently viewed, on the shop. Effort S. Backend: none.**
A strip of the last six products opened, kept in `localStorage`, shown on
`/store` and at the foot of a product page. No API, no cookie, no consent
question — it never leaves the browser.

**P8 — Validate on blur, not only on submit. Effort S. Backend: none.**
Contact, ticket, registration and checkout all validate on the server
(correctly — that stays the boundary) and say nothing until the round trip
comes back. Mirror the *shape* rules on blur — an email with no `@`, a
phone that is letters, a GSTIN of the wrong length — under the field, using
the same `Field` error slot. Never on every keystroke, and never for
anything the server has to decide (a code that exists, an address on file).

**P9 — Product page gallery: open the picture. Effort S. Backend: none.**
Thumbnails swap the main image in place (right), and that is all. The
gallery's lightbox already exists (`gallery.tsx`); let the main image open
it, with the thumbnails as the strip. A 300px well is small for a rack
switch's port layout.

**P10 — Fewer, better reveals. Effort S. Backend: none.**
Every section on every page fades up as it scrolls in. It is well built
(vertical only, no clipping, reduced-motion aware) and it is also the single
most recognisable "generated site" tell. Keep one orchestrated moment per
page — the hero — and let sections simply be there. This is taste rather
than usability, so it is last.

## The shop

**S1 — Quantity and removal on the cart page should not reload.** Effort S.
Backend: none. The basket already announces `tw:cart` and the indicator
listens. Make the cart page's quantity stepper optimistic — update the line
and the totals immediately, reconcile with the action's reply — and animate
a removed line out over `--duration-exit` rather than snapping. The
checkout stays exactly as it is: it re-prices everything under a lock.

**S2 — An order page that reads as a timeline.** Effort M. Backend: none.
Orders carry `placed_at`, `paid_at`, the status and the dispatch stamps;
the order page lists them as fields. Draw them as a four-step line —
Placed · Paid · Packed · Dispatched — with the current step live and the
tracking link on the last. For a cash-on-delivery order the second step
reads "Pay on delivery" rather than pretending. The same component in the
console's order screen, so the desk and the customer see one picture.

**S3 — "Add to basket" should show what it did.** Effort S. Backend: none.
The toast is there (good). Add the one thing people look for: the basket
count in the header changing *while* the toast shows, with the wiggle that
already exists — and on the product page, swap the button to "Added ·
View basket" for three seconds.

**S4 — Stock words, not a boolean.** Effort S. Backend: none.
`in_stock` and `allow_oversell` are already the API's answer; the product
page says "In stock". Say "Back-ordered — ships in 7–10 days" when oversell
is on and stock is out; it is the honest reading of the flag and the
difference between a sale and an abandoned basket. No count is published
(the rule stands).

## The customer portal

**T1 — The dashboard tiles should be filters.** Effort S. Backend: none.
"1 In progress" is a number on a card. Make every tile a link to
`/portal/tickets?status=…` — the list already takes the parameter.

**T2 — Show the customer the clock.** Effort S. Backend: none.
The API sends `due_at` on every ticket and the portal renders only
"Overdue". Show "Reply expected by Thu 3:30 pm" on the ticket and in the
list, counting down while it is ahead and plainly red once it is behind. A
customer with an SLA is paying for that number; hiding it is a missed
reassurance on every ticket that is on time.

**T3 — The thread should notice a reply.** Effort M. Backend: none (later:
Web Push).** The thread is server-rendered and never refreshes. While the
tab is visible, call `router.refresh()` every 60 seconds and, when the
message count grows, scroll a "New reply from Technoware" pill into view
rather than jumping. It is not real-time and does not need to be: a support
thread moves in minutes. Web Push is the upgrade path, and a larger piece.

**T4 — Suggest articles while the subject is typed.** Effort M. Backend:
none.** The new-ticket form points at the knowledge base *before* the form
(good). Better: as the subject is typed, query `/knowledge-base?q=` (300ms
debounce, min 3 characters) and show three matches under the field — "This
might answer it". Every article opened from there is a ticket not raised,
and it is the deflection the brief asked for made live.

**T5 — A status history the customer can see.** Effort M. Backend: S.
The desk sees the ticket's event trail; the customer sees a badge. Expose a
public subset of events (status changes and assignment, never internal
notes — the same `publicMessages` boundary) and render a compact vertical
timeline above the conversation: Raised → Assigned to Arjun → In progress.

**T6 — Image attachments inline.** Effort S. Backend: none.
An attachment in the thread is a filename. A picture of a blinking switch
is most of what a customer can tell you; render image attachments as
thumbnails that open the lightbox, through the same authorised stream.

## The console

**C1 — Filters apply as they change.** Effort M. Backend: none.
The same finding as P1, sixteen times. `FilterBar` is one component, so
this is one change: selects apply on change, text on a debounce, the URL
stays the state, and the Apply button stays for keyboards and no-JS.

**C2 — A command palette on Ctrl+K.** Effort L. Backend: M.
The sidebar has a "Filter…" box that narrows the menu. Grow it into the
console's front door: type a ticket reference, a customer, a product, a
post, and jump — plus the actions ("New post", "Settings → Mail"). One
endpoint, `GET /admin/search?q=`, fanning out to the indexes that already
take `?q=`, five per type, role-filtered by the same middleware the routes
use. For a tool worked at for hours, this is the change that changes the
most keystrokes.

**C3 — Bulk actions on the ticket queue.** Effort M. Backend: S.
The queue has inline status and assignee selects per row (good) and no way
to take five tickets at once. A tick column, a bar that appears with a
selection ("Assign to me", "Set status"), and `POST /admin/tickets/bulk`
taking `ids[]` and the same two fields, writing one event per ticket. The
media library's selection bar is the pattern; reuse it.

**C4 — Every dashboard number is a door.** Effort S. Backend: none.
The store overview already links its "attention" figures to the filtered
list; the main dashboard's tiles do not. "5 Overdue tickets" should open
`/admin/tickets?overdue=1`. Same for leads and enquiries.

**C5 — Leads: act on the row.** Effort M. Backend: none.
A lead row shows Unassigned and New and offers nothing until it is opened.
Add "Take it" (assign to me) and a status menu on the row, the way tickets
have — the API already returns `allowed_next` per lead, so the menu offers
only what a `PATCH` will accept.

**C6 — Warn before an in-app navigation loses a form.** Effort M. Backend:
none.** `FormActions` warns before a refresh (`beforeunload`) and cannot see
a sidebar click. Intercept `Link` clicks inside the console while a form
is dirty — a small context the sidebar and the form both use — and show
the same "Discard changes?" dialog. Add Ctrl/Cmd+S to submit the form
while at it; editors expect it in a body editor.

**C7 — Something new since you were last here.** Effort M. Backend: S.
Nothing in the console says a ticket or lead arrived. A count on Tickets
and Leads in the sidebar, polled from `/admin/dashboard` every 60 seconds
while the tab is visible, keyed to a `since` the API takes — and the tab
title carrying the number ("(2) Console"), which is what people actually
glance at.

**C8 — Sort by clicking the column.** Effort S. Backend: none for the
lists that already accept `?sort=`; S for the rest.** Sorting is a select in
the filter bar. Make the column headers the control, with the arrow, and
keep the select for phones where the headers are gone.

**C9 — Keyboard in the media grid.** Effort S. Backend: none.
Arrow keys move between tiles, Space opens the preview, Enter opens the
details, Delete sends to the bin (with the confirmation). The grid is the
one console screen worked with pictures rather than words, and it is the one
that rewards the keyboard most.

**C10 — Autosave drafts for the long forms.** Effort M. Backend: none.
Products, solutions and posts are nine-field, four-tab forms. Keep a draft
of the form's `FormData` in `localStorage` keyed by route, every ten seconds
while dirty, and offer "Restore what you were typing" on return. It never
touches the server, so nothing can be half-saved.

## Backend affordances the API does not have yet

Everything above that needs the API, in one place, smallest first.

- `POST /knowledge-base/{slug}/helpful` — throttled, 204 always, increments
  `helpful_count`. (P4)
- `POST /admin/tickets/bulk` — `ids[]`, `status`, `assigned_to`; one event
  per ticket; refuses an illegal transition per ticket and reports which. (C3)
- `GET /admin/dashboard?since=<iso>` — the existing counts plus
  `new_since` for tickets and leads. (C7)
- A public `events` subset on `GET /tickets/{reference}` — status and
  assignment changes only, never notes. (T5)
- `GET /admin/search?q=` — five per type across tickets, customers, leads,
  products, posts, pages; role-filtered. (C2)
- `?sort=` on the admin indexes that lack it. (C8)

Nothing else on the list needs a byte changed in Laravel.

## How to read the effort

Every item was sized against the primitives that exist: `FilterBar`,
`Form`, `Field`, `Modal`, the toast, the listbox in `store-search.tsx`, the
selection bar in the media library, `useSaveStatus`, the lightbox. Where an
item says "reuse", the point is not thrift — a second listbox or a second
selection bar is the two-definitions drift this codebase keeps catching.
The numbers assume that.

The screenshots the audit was made from are throwaway (`web/scripts/_shots`)
and are not committed.
