# SEO audit and enhancement plan — 2026-09-18

A read of the whole codebase against a technical-SEO checklist, then a plan
for the SEO modules with the AI assistant first, as asked. Everything in
"What is already right" was verified in code rather than assumed; everything
in "Findings" names the file. The site is not live, so nothing here is a
ranking measurement — that starts the day Search Console has data.

## 2. Findings

Ordered by what they cost. None is a crawl-blocker; three change what
Google indexes, the rest change how well.

### F1 — Paginated listings canonicalise to page 1 (medium)

`/blog?page=2`, `/products?page=2`, `/store?page=2`, `/knowledge-base?page=2`
and the category listings all export a static `metadata` whose canonical is
the bare path. Google ignores `rel=prev/next` since 2019 and asks for a
**self-referencing canonical on each paginated page**; canonicalising page 2
to page 1 tells it page 2 is a duplicate, so the products only reachable
from page 2 onward are discovered through the sitemap alone and the listing
pages themselves consolidate into one. `generateMetadata` reading
`searchParams.page` and appending `?page=N` to the canonical (and leaving
`?q=`/`?brand=` variants `noindex, follow`) is the fix; the sitemap stays as
it is.

Files: `app/(marketing)/blog/page.tsx`, `products/page.tsx`, `store/page.tsx`,
`knowledge-base/page.tsx`, `products/[slug]/page.tsx` (category mode),
`store/categories/[slug]/page.tsx`.

### F2 — `lastmod` in the sitemap is "now" for most URLs (medium)

`sitemap.ts` defaults `lastModified` to `new Date()`; only posts, articles
and vacancies pass a date, and they pass `published_at` rather than
`updated_at`. A sitemap whose every `lastmod` is the build time is one
Google learns to ignore — and the whole point of the field is to get a
changed page recrawled early. The public index resources carry `updated_at`
(pages do; products, solutions and case studies can), so pass it through and
drop the default. For the static routes, use the newest `updated_at` among
the records the page lists.

### F3 — `robots.txt` has no crawl-budget or AI-crawler policy (low, but decide it)

`app/robots.ts` disallows `/portal/`, `/api/` and `/theme-preview/` only.
`/admin/`, `/cart`, `/checkout`, `/order/`, `/search` and `/embed/` are
`noindex` and reachable, so a crawler fetches each to learn it is not wanted.
Add them to `disallow`. Separately — the AI half of this report — state a
policy for `GPTBot`, `ClaudeBot`, `PerplexityBot`, `Google-Extended`,
`CCBot`, `Applebot-Extended`: the default is "allowed", which is the right
call for a business that wants to be cited by assistants, and saying so in
the file is what makes it a decision rather than an accident.

### F4 — The `Organization` graph is thin for a knowledge panel (low)

`lib/seo.tsx` emits name, url, description, logo, one address line and a
`ContactPoint`. Missing, and all available from settings today: `sameAs`
(the three social URLs — the one place those links do SEO work), `@id` so
the `Article.publisher` and `Service.provider` nodes reference the same
entity instead of repeating it, `foundingDate`, `areaServed`, and a
`PostalAddress` split into locality/region/postcode rather than one
`streetAddress` string. `WebSite` has no `potentialAction: SearchAction`
though the site has `/search?q=` — that is the sitelinks search box.

### F5 — Article pages do not carry the Open Graph article fields (low)

`buildMetadata` accepts `type: "article"` but never sets
`publishedTime`, `modifiedTime`, `authors` or `tags` on `openGraph`. Blog
posts, knowledge articles and case studies all have the data. Cheap, and it
is what social previews and some aggregators read for the byline and date.

### F6 — Nothing tells search engines about a change until the next crawl (low)

Publishing a post or renaming a product updates the cache tag and the
sitemap, and then waits. **IndexNow** (Bing, Yandex, Seznam, Naver — and
Bing feeds Copilot/ChatGPT search) is one `POST` per changed URL with a key
file at the root; the console's save actions already call `updateTag`, which
is exactly where a ping belongs. Google has no equivalent beyond the sitemap,
so F2 is the Google half of this.

### F7 — No `llms.txt` and no answer-first summaries (AI search)

Nothing on the site addresses generative engines directly. See §3.

### Not findings, on purpose

- No `Offer` price on the marketing catalogue — the brief rules out selling; the store carries real prices.
- No `AggregateRating`/`Review` — nothing collects them; fabricating is the worst thing in the file.
- No hreflang — one language, one region.
- The four "structurally guaranteed" checks (H1, canonical, schema, breadcrumbs) are deliberately not in the score — they cannot fail here.

## 3. AI SEO — the plan, first target

Two halves, both called "AI SEO" and both wanted: **the assistant** (what
the console does with a model) and **generative-engine optimisation** (what
the site says to assistants that answer questions with it).

### 3a. The assistant — from six buttons to a working loop

What exists: per-record `generate`, `analyze`, `improve`, `faq`,
`internal_links`, `schema`, each a button on the SEO panel, suggest-only,
capped, logged. What it lacks is *scale* and *feedback*: an editor presses
one button on one record and reads one answer.

1. **Bulk runs from the overview.** `/admin/seo` already lists every record
   with its failed checks and `?check=` filters. Add "Draft descriptions for
   these 23" and "Draft titles for these 9" — one queued job per record
   through the existing `SeoAssistant`, results landing as `pending`
   suggestions the editor accepts one by one (never applied by the job:
   the module's rule). The daily cap already bounds the bill; the job
   respects it and stops.
2. **Keyword targets before generation.** `seo.secondary_keywords` exists;
   the focus keyword is scored (`keyword_*`, five checks) but the assistant
   is not told which keyword the record is chasing beyond what is stored.
   Add `keyword_suggest`: from the record's body, the catalogue and the
   business context, propose a focus keyword and three secondaries with the
   intent named — the `keyword-deep-dive` shape, without a SERP tool. Every
   later action then optimises *toward* the stored keyword, so the score's
   five keyword checks and the assistant agree.
3. **Content briefs from the chatbot's unanswered list.** The assistant's
   `/admin/chat/unanswered` groups the questions the website could not
   answer — a list of demand with no supply. One action: "Brief a knowledge
   article for this question" → a draft KB article (title, outline, FAQ
   pairs, the internal links from the numbered list) as a `draft` record an
   editor finishes. Nothing else on the roadmap turns measured demand into
   pages.
4. **Alt text for the media library.** `image_alt` is a score check and a
   real accessibility gap; the library holds the file and knows which
   records use it. A vision-capable call per image (the same provider,
   `response_format` json) proposing alt text, written to the media row on
   accept — one field, one endpoint, the pattern `SeoAiController` already
   has.
5. **Search Console in the loop.** Once live: the GSC API (queries,
   impressions, CTR per page) as an `integrations` credential; the overview
   gains "impressions, no clicks" as a filter and `improve` is told the
   queries the page already ranks for. This is the one item that needs the
   site to be live, and it is the one that makes the rest measurable.
6. **A suggestion diff, and acceptance stats.** The panel shows a
   suggestion; showing it *against* the current value, and reporting per
   model what percentage was accepted, is how the client learns which model
   to keep paying for — the `seo_suggestions` table already stores both.

### 3b. Generative-engine optimisation — being the source an assistant cites

Assistants answer from pages that state facts plainly, early, and with
structure. Most of the site's content already does; what is missing is the
scaffolding that lets a crawler find and quote it.

1. **`/llms.txt` and `/llms-full.txt`** (the emerging convention): a
   Markdown index of what the company does, its solutions, services,
   categories and the knowledge base, with one-line summaries and canonical
   URLs, generated from the API the way `sitemap.ts` is. `llms-full.txt`
   inlines the KB articles' text. A route handler each, ISR-cached.
2. **AI-crawler policy in `robots.txt`** — F3. Allow, explicitly.
3. **Answer-first blocks.** Every solution, service and KB article gets a
   two-sentence "In short" at the top — a setting-free, editor-written
   field (`summary` already exists on most; render it as the first
   paragraph, not only in cards) — plus `speakable` in the structured data
   pointing at it. Assistants and featured snippets both lift from the
   first 40–60 words.
4. **More FAQ, where the questions are real.** `FAQPage` already renders
   for records with FAQs; the assistant's `faq` action and the chatbot's
   unanswered list are the two sources of *real* questions. Wire 3a.3 so
   every published answer to an unanswered question also lands as an FAQ
   pair on the page it belongs to.
5. **Entity clarity.** F4 — `sameAs`, `@id`, split address — is what lets
   an assistant resolve "Technoware" to one entity rather than a name.
6. **Citable facts.** Case studies carry `results` figures; render them
   with the unit and the date in text (not only as counters), because a
   number an assistant can quote with its source is the one it quotes.
7. **Keep the API readable.** `/api/v1` is public JSON of the same content;
   nothing to do here except not block it for AI crawlers if they ever
   reach it — they fetch HTML.

## 3d. Done on 2026-09-18 (same day)

Steps 1, 2, 3 and 7 of the order of work, plus the first half of 4:

- **F1** — `listingMetadata()` in `lib/seo.tsx`; `/blog`, `/products`, `/store`, `/knowledge-base`, `/blog/category/{slug}` and the category listings under `/products/{slug}` carry `?page=N` canonicals and " — page N" titles; search, facet and archive views are `noindex, follow`. Verified in the served HTML.
- **F2** — every public resource carries `updated_at`; the sitemap uses it, an index page takes the newest among its records, and a page with no record behind it sends no `lastmod` at all (116 of the URLs carry one; About and Contact do not).
- **F3** — `robots.ts` disallows every `noindex` route and names thirteen AI crawlers as allowed.
- **F4** — `Organization` carries `@id`, `sameAs`, a parsed `PostalAddress`; `WebSite` links to it; the API's `publisher`/`isPartOf` nodes point at both. No `SearchAction`: Google retired the sitelinks search box in 2024.
- **F5** — posts, articles and case studies send the Open Graph article fields.
- **F6** — IndexNow: `App\Support\IndexNow`, `PingIndexNow`, the `indexnow` settings group, the key file at `/indexnow/{key}.txt`. Off until launch.
- **3b.1** — `/llms.txt` and `/llms-full.txt` from `lib/llms.ts`.
- **3a.1** — bulk runs: `POST /admin/seo/ai/bulk`, `RunSeoSuggestion`, "Draft for these N" on the overview, `ai_pending` per row and `?ai=pending` as the review queue.

Later the same day — **3a.2** (the `keywords` action, and every action told the record's stored keywords to keep) and **3a.3 with 3b.4** (`ArticleBrief`: "Draft an article" on an unanswered group writes a draft knowledge article with a Q&A block and `[CHECK: …]` holes; `POST /admin/chat/unanswered/brief`).

And **3a.4** (`AltText`: "Suggest alt text" in a picture's Edit dialog, `POST /admin/media/{id}/alt-suggest`, the picture sent as a `data:` URL).

And the rest, the same evening — **3b.3** (`speakable` on every article and service graph, naming the `h1` and the `.lede` every theme renders, so the answer-first sentence a page opens with is the one an assistant may quote), **3a.6** (`meta.ai.usage`: what each model suggested, applied and rejected over ninety days with an acceptance rate that is null until something is decided; and the panel shows a suggestion against the value the form holds), and **3a.5** (`App\Support\Seo\SearchConsole`: a service-account key under Settings → API keys, a Search column and a "Shown, never opened" filter on the overview, the queries a page already ranks for in the assistant's context, a Test button that reports Google's own words). The whole plan is built; what remains needs the site to be live: connecting a real property and reading what the figures say.

The plan is complete as of 2026-09-18.

## 4. What to measure once live

Search Console: impressions and clicks per page type (solution, product,
KB, landing); the share of pages "Crawled — currently not indexed"; sitemap
read status and whether `lastmod` moves. Bing Webmaster Tools for IndexNow
receipts. For the AI half: referrals from `chatgpt.com`, `perplexity.ai`,
`copilot.microsoft.com` in analytics (they carry a referrer), and periodic
manual checks of what assistants say about the company — the only test of
3b that exists today.
